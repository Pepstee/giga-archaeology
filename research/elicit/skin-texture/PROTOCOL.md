# Skin texture, collagen stimulation and scar-treatment evidence map

Status: **all 37 refined queries acquired and deterministically projected; negative controls
verified; human screening, search saturation, full-text appraisal and synthesis pending**.

Protocol version: `giga.skin-texture-evidence-map.v1`  
Protocol date: 2026-07-28

## Objective

Build a reproducible evidence map of interventions claimed to improve visible or objectively
measured skin texture, photoaging, dermal collagen, and scars. Rank interventions only after
source-level appraisal, from strongest net evidence to ineffective, unsupported, commonly
misused, or net harmful.

This is an evidence-acquisition protocol, not medical advice. The resulting module output cannot
exceed `A0` planning authority and cannot select a prescription, procedure, device setting, or
personal treatment without a separate clinical decision.

## Why this is not one question

The campaign keeps these conditions separate:

1. **Photoaging and general facial texture** — roughness, fine/coarse wrinkles, elasticity,
   laxity, pore appearance, dyspigmentation only when reported alongside texture.
2. **Atrophic acne scars** — ice-pick, rolling and boxcar scars, including tethering and depth.
3. **Other atrophic scars** — surgical, traumatic and varicella scars where evidence exists.
4. **Hypertrophic scars** — raised scars that remain within the original wound.
5. **Keloids** — raised scars extending beyond the original wound, with recurrence as a primary
   outcome.

Combining these populations would make the ranking clinically misleading.

## Primary questions

1. Which preventive, topical, oral, injectable, needling, energy-based and surgical
   interventions improve validated texture/photoaging outcomes versus placebo, no treatment or
   an active comparator?
2. Which interventions produce human evidence of dermal remodeling or new collagen, and does
   that translate into visible, durable benefit?
3. Which treatments work best for each atrophic-scar subtype?
4. Which treatments reduce hypertrophic-scar or keloid severity and recurrence?
5. Which popular claims have negative, null, low-certainty, surrogate-only or
   industry-dependent evidence?
6. Which interventions work for a different outcome than the one advertised, create only a
   transient optical/hydration effect, or are commonly used with the wrong indication,
   technique, dose, interval or combination?

## Eligible evidence

### Include

- Human studies.
- Systematic reviews, meta-analyses, evidence-based guidelines and consensus algorithms.
- Randomised or controlled clinical trials for efficacy.
- Prospective comparative cohorts when controlled trials are unavailable.
- Large observational studies, registries and case series for uncommon harms, recurrence and
  durability.
- Studies reporting at least one prespecified outcome and enough detail to identify population,
  intervention, comparator and follow-up.
- All publication years.
- All countries and skin phototypes.
- Non-English studies when a provider supplies usable English metadata; record the language
  limitation and verify decisive claims against the full text or a qualified translation.

### Exclude from efficacy ranking

- Animal, in-vitro or ex-vivo studies. Preserve them only in a mechanism appendix.
- Product marketing, clinic pages, testimonials and uncontrolled before/after advertising.
- Expert opinion without traceable clinical evidence.
- Pigment-only, acne-only or hydration-only studies unless texture, scar or collagen outcomes
  are also measured.
- Studies where the intervention cannot be distinguished from a multi-ingredient combination.
- Duplicate publications of the same population, except when they add longer follow-up or a
  distinct prespecified outcome.

## Intervention taxonomy

### Prevention and foundational care

- Broad-spectrum sunscreen and other photoprotection.
- Moisturisers, barrier repair and occlusion.
- Smoking cessation, sleep, nutrition and other lifestyle claims when clinically tested.

### Topical medication and cosmeceuticals

- Tretinoin, tazarotene, adapalene and topical isotretinoin.
- Retinol, retinaldehyde, retinyl esters and other retinoid precursors.
- Alpha- and beta-hydroxy acids, polyhydroxy acids and chemical-peel agents.
- Vitamin C, niacinamide, azelaic acid and antioxidants.
- Peptides, growth factors, topical collagen, hyaluronic acid and ceramides.
- Topical oestrogens and hormonal agents where studied.
- Silicone, vitamin E, onion extract, imiquimod and other scar products.

### Oral interventions

- Hydrolysed collagen/collagen peptides.
- Protein, amino acids, vitamins, antioxidants and mixed nutricosmetics.
- Prescription systemic agents used for pathological scarring.

### Needling, energy and resurfacing

- Standard microneedling and home dermarolling as separate exposures.
- Radiofrequency microneedling; monopolar, bipolar and fractional radiofrequency.
- Ablative and fractional CO2 or Er:YAG laser resurfacing.
- Non-ablative fractional lasers and other vascular/pigment lasers when texture is measured.
- Pulsed-dye laser, intense pulsed light, photodynamic therapy and LED/red-light devices.
- Ultrasound/HIFU and plasma devices.
- Dermabrasion, microdermabrasion and chemical peels.

### Injectables, regenerative and surgical interventions

- Hyaluronic-acid fillers, PMMA and autologous fat.
- Poly-L-lactic acid and calcium-hydroxylapatite biostimulators.
- Platelet-rich plasma/fibrin and combinations.
- Polynucleotides/PDRN, exosomes, conditioned media and stem-cell-derived products.
- Subcision, punch excision/elevation/grafting and scar revision.
- Intralesional corticosteroid, 5-fluorouracil, bleomycin, verapamil and botulinum toxin.
- Cryotherapy, pressure therapy, surgery with adjuvant treatment and radiotherapy for keloids.

## Outcomes

### Efficacy

- Validated wrinkle, photoaging, texture or roughness scale.
- Optical profilometry/visiometry, three-dimensional imaging or standardized blinded grading.
- Validated scar scales: ECCA, Goodman and Baron, Vancouver Scar Scale, POSAS or equivalent.
- At least 50% improvement and at least one-grade improvement where reported.
- Scar height, thickness, pliability, vascularity, pigmentation, pain and pruritus.
- Recurrence, especially at 6, 12 and 24 months for keloids.
- Patient-reported satisfaction and dermatology quality of life.

### Collagen and mechanism

- Histology, confocal microscopy or ultrasound evidence of dermal thickness/density.
- Procollagen, collagen subtype, elastin and matrix-metalloproteinase outcomes.
- Mechanistic outcomes never substitute for a clinical outcome.

### Burden and safety

- Pain, erythema, oedema, infection, dermatitis and treatment discontinuation.
- Post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation, hypopigmentation and phototype-specific outcomes.
- Persistent erythema, burns, fat loss, nerve or vascular injury, new scarring and keloid
  formation.
- Downtime, number of sessions, durability, recurrence, clinician dependency and cost.
- Funding source, conflicts of interest and manufacturer involvement.

## Mandatory subgroup extraction

- Fitzpatrick phototype, ethnicity and post-inflammatory-hyperpigmentation risk.
- Age, sex, anatomical site and baseline severity.
- Atrophic-scar subtype and degree of tethering.
- Hypertrophic scar versus keloid, location, age and previous recurrence.
- Device name, wavelength, fluence, density, depth, temperature, passes and session interval.
- Topical concentration, vehicle, frequency, duration and adherence.
- Operator qualifications and whether treatment was clinic-based or home-administered.

## Misconception and misuse classification

Every challenged claim must be placed in exactly one category:

1. `contradicted` — reliable comparative human evidence shows no benefit or worse outcomes.
2. `unsupported` — no adequate comparative human evidence.
3. `surrogate_only` — laboratory or collagen-marker change without demonstrated visible benefit.
4. `transient_not_remodelling` — short-lived hydration, swelling, vasodilation or optical
   smoothing marketed as structural change.
5. `wrong_outcome` — effective for another outcome, but not the claimed texture/scar outcome.
6. `wrong_indication` — a useful treatment applied to the wrong scar subtype or condition.
7. `wrong_protocol` — plausible treatment undermined by unsafe depth, dose, interval,
   combination, aftercare or home use.
8. `evidence_conflicted` — apparent benefit disappears in high-quality or non-industry-funded
   evidence.
9. `unresolved` — evidence is genuinely mixed or too heterogeneous for a truthful conclusion.

## Appraisal and ranking rules

Search results remain third-party claims. Each decisive source must receive a
`giga.scientific-appraisal.v1` appraisal based on the actual full text, not the abstract alone.

Interventions will be ranked independently for each condition and outcome:

- **A — strongest net evidence:** consistent controlled evidence, clinically meaningful benefit,
  acceptable harms and useful durability.
- **B — moderate/conditional:** benefit is likely but smaller, less durable, parameter-dependent,
  population-dependent or supported by fewer high-quality trials.
- **C — limited/emerging:** promising signal with important bias, imprecision, heterogeneity or
  short follow-up.
- **D — insufficient:** no reliable basis to rank effectiveness.
- **E — ineffective for the claimed outcome:** adequate evidence shows no meaningful benefit.
- **F — avoid/net harm:** harms or misuse outweigh demonstrated benefit for the claimed use.

No ranking may be based on study design alone. It must record effect size, uncertainty,
applicability, durability, adverse events, conflicts and contradictory findings. Treatments in
different condition groups must not share a single league table.

## Search and saturation procedure

1. Run every query in `CAMPAIGN.json`.
2. Preserve sanitised provider request envelopes and complete raw responses in the
   scientific-evidence ledger.
3. Deduplicate by DOI, PMID, trial identifier and then normalized title/year.
4. Use high-quality reviews and guidelines as seed sources; recover eligible primary trials
   cited by them.
5. Run forward searches for newer trials and systematic reviews.
6. Search ClinicalTrials.gov directly for completed, terminated and unpublished studies.
7. Add candidate interventions discovered during screening, with a logged protocol amendment.
8. Repeat targeted searches until two consecutive passes produce no new treatment family and
   less than 1% new eligible records.
9. Record a PRISMA-style flow and a search-completion date. Never claim perpetual completeness.

## Current acquisition and access boundary

The canonical discovery stack is:

- Europe PMC and PubMed for primary biomedical discovery;
- Semantic Scholar for related-work and citation-graph expansion;
- optional OpenAlex expansion with a free API key;
- ClinicalTrials.gov for trial-registry evidence;
- Crossref for DOI verification;
- Unpaywall, Europe PMC/PMC, lawful repositories, institutional access and author manuscripts
  for full-text resolution.

Sci-Hub, Z-Library, credential sharing and paywall circumvention are outside the acquisition
boundary. A paper already held lawfully may be registered by local file hash. If lawful full
text cannot be obtained, it remains unavailable and cannot satisfy `full_text_verified`.
