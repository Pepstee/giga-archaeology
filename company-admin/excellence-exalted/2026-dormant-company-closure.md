## Deadline correction — what actually costs money

The civil late-filing penalty is measured from the accounts due date of 8 July 2026, not from the
statutory notice. Verified against GOV.UK guidance updated 16 January 2026:

| Delivered | Penalty |
|---|---:|
| by 8 August 2026 (not more than 1 month late) | £150 |
| 9 August – 8 October 2026 | £375 |
| 9 October 2026 – 8 January 2027 | £750 |
| later | £1,500 |

So 6 August is the enforcement date in the notice, but **8 August is the date with a price on it**,
and the difference is £225.

Appealing is not a viable route. GOV.UK lists, among grounds unlikely to succeed: that the company
is dormant, that these are the first accounts, and that the directors live or were travelling
overseas. All three apply here.

## Important position

The company being dormant does not remove its Companies House filing duties. Its first accounts are already late. There is no guaranteed zero-penalty route now.

Do not send the previously drafted “I want to keep my company” email. That draft was removed on 30 July 2026.

Do not pay Corporation Tax merely because HMRC issued a reminder. A company that never traded and had no income will ordinarily owe no Corporation Tax, but HMRC must be told and any Notice to Deliver a Company Tax Return must still be answered.

## Lowest-cost orderly route

### 1. Confirm genuine dormancy

The company must have had no significant accounting transactions. Permitted exceptions include:

- the £1 paid for the subscriber share on incorporation;
- Companies House filing fees;
- late-filing penalties.

Confirm separately whether it ever:

- received sales, grants, interest or any other income;
- bought or sold goods or services;
- paid wages, teachers or contractors;
- paid for advertising;
- held an income-producing asset;
- incurred transactions through a company bank account.

If any of these occurred, stop treating it as dormant and prepare appropriate accounts and tax records.

### 2. Resolve HMRC status immediately — submitted

The official HMRC dormancy notification was submitted on 30 July 2026:

https://www.gov.uk/tell-hmrc-your-company-is-dormant-for-corporation-tax

The HMRC reminder identifies a short accounting period ending 31 October 2025. Check the company's Business Tax Account for every Notice to Deliver a Company Tax Return, not only this reminder.

If HMRC issued a Notice to Deliver:

- a nil Company Tax Return may still be required for the period on the notice;
- telling HMRC the company is dormant does not automatically cancel an existing notice;
- obtain written confirmation of what returns, if any, remain outstanding before dissolution.

Retain the acknowledgement PDF and confirmation email. The notification does not override a separate Notice to Deliver a Company Tax Return if HMRC has issued one; check subsequent correspondence for any such requirement.

### 3. Apply for voluntary strike-off

Official service:

https://www.gov.uk/strike-off-your-company-from-companies-register/apply-to-strike-off

Current published fee: £18.

Before applying:

- ensure the company has no debts other than any unresolved filing penalty;
- close any company bank account and remove any remaining assets;
- resolve HMRC's Corporation Tax record;
- identify everyone legally entitled to receive a copy of the application.

The majority of directors must approve and sign. If there is one director, that director applies.

Within 7 days after applying, send a copy of the application to all legally required interested parties, including shareholders, creditors and relevant authorities where applicable.

HMRC or another creditor can object until unfinished tax or debt matters are resolved.

### 4. Handle the overdue dormant accounts notice

Companies House states that dormant companies must still file annual accounts. The original deadline has passed, so filing now normally triggers the automatic private-company late-filing penalty:

- no more than one month late: £150;
- more than one month but no more than three months late: £375;
- higher bands apply after that.

The statutory notice demands acceptable accounts by 6 August 2026 and warns of prosecution/disqualification if ignored. Voluntary strike-off is not a mechanism for evading an existing legal obligation or debt.

Contact Companies House promptly and state that:

- the company has never traded;
- the director intends to apply for voluntary strike-off;
- first dormant accounts are already overdue;
- clarification is requested on whether Companies House requires those dormant accounts before dissolution and whether a late-filing penalty has already been imposed.

Companies House:

- Email: enquiries@companieshouse.gov.uk
- Telephone: 0303 1234 500

Do not use the subject “I want to keep my company.”

### 6 August 2026 status

6 August is the final date stated in the 16 July statutory notice for Companies House to receive acceptable accounts. It is not an automatic dissolution date and no event at midnight erases the company's obligations.

If acceptable dormant accounts are not received by the end of 6 August, Companies House can move from warning to enforcement: it may pursue the director for the criminal filing offence and/or begin registrar-led strike-off. Those processes are not necessarily immediate on 7 August, and compulsory strike-off does not cancel an existing late-filing penalty or other company debt.

The separate civil late-filing penalty is measured from the original 8 July deadline. Accounts received no more than one month late attract the £150 private-company band; more than one month late attracts £375. On the published month-counting rule, 8 August is the corresponding one-month point, so the safest course is to file acceptable dormant accounts online immediately, preferably on 6 August and certainly before crossing that point. Filing requires the company's Companies House authentication code; it does not depend on the GOV.UK One Login SMS used for the strike-off application.

Immediate order of operations:

1. File dormant accounts online on 6 August if the authentication code is available.
2. If it is unavailable, contact Companies House the same day on 0303 1234 500 and at enquiries@companieshouse.gov.uk, record the contact, and request/recover the code; Companies House states that replacement codes are posted and cannot be given by email or phone.
3. Apply for voluntary strike-off as soon as GOV.UK One Login access is recovered, while resolving any penalty or other outstanding obligation rather than assuming dissolution will remove it.
