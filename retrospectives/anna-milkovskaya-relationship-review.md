# Anna Milkovskaya: relationship evidence review

**Prepared:** 22 July 2026  
**Primary evidence:** native Telegram export, 20 October 2024–7 July 2025  
**Underlying episode:** winter and summer 2022, reconstructed retrospectively

## Evidence boundary

The native Telegram thread contains 449 messages: 271 from Anna and 178 from Artiom. Almost the
entire live exchange took place on 20, 22 and 23 October 2024. Anna then sent a 21,005-character
letter in thirteen messages on 14 April 2025. On 7 July 2025 Artiom sent three Word documents —
`Объяснение произошедшего.docx`, `Архитектура Психики - Артём.docx`, and
`Архитектура Психики - Анна.docx` — followed by “Конечно многое спекуляция, но моё понимание
совпадает.” The export names but does not contain those attachments. Their surrounding ChatGPT
history preserves drafts and conceptual material, but AI-generated psychological language is not
independent evidence.

The 2022 WhatsApp/VK record is absent. The underlying events therefore come mainly from Anna's
retrospective account. Artiom did, however, corroborate several concrete memories during the 2024
exchange after initially describing the summer as “three walks.” Interpretations of motive remain
less certain than events.

## Short conclusion

Anna was a childhood friend who became intensely and enduringly attached during a brief but dense
reconnection in 2022. The evidence supports mutual flirtation and emotional intimacy, but not a
defined relationship. Artiom experienced the episode as a rebound and later as a minor chapter;
Anna experienced it as the discovery of reciprocal love and the beginning of a shared future.

The central injury was not simply rejection. It was the sequence of warmth, ambiguity, departure,
apparent disappearance, fear that Artiom had died, later discovery that he was alive, and continued
refusal to give an unambiguous account. Anna amplified the episode through idealisation and years
of rumination, but Artiom prolonged the ambiguity and then minimised the shared history. Both
processes mattered.

## Reconstructed sequence

### Childhood friendship and winter reclassification

Anna says they had known one another since second grade and shared the same friendship group,
“Союз.” She had categorised him firmly as a friend. During a winter 2022 visit to Chișinău, they
spent a day walking together. She noticed an unexpectedly strong physical and emotional response,
but told herself that he was merely a distant friend and the day had no romantic future.

### Summer 2022: mutual cues, asymmetric meaning

When Anna returned from Rome, they began spending extensive time together. Her list includes
walking through several Chișinău districts and forests, a Zdob și Zdub concert at La Izvor,
meeting her friend and the friend's near-boyfriend, a visit to the stable, prolonged embraces
outside her building, and sitting together until 7 a.m. before his departure. She says she skipped
training and accumulated a month's normal walking distance. Artiom remembered some specific scenes
when prompted, including the friend's sexual joke and the stable.

Anna interpreted his physical affection and focused attention as reciprocal attraction. She also
states that he invited her to Transnistria, but she declined because further intimacy would deepen
her attachment. Artiom's 2024 explanation was “rebound”: he had not emotionally recovered from
Alexandra, became close to Anna, and believed he stopped early enough. That establishes that the
episode was not purely imaginary on Anna's side, while not establishing that he intended a
relationship.

### Departure, withdrawal and disappearance

According to Anna, warmth continued at first after he left, but Artiom increasingly said that
long-distance relationships did not work and the episode should remain in the past. He later sent
a late-night farewell message after an extended absence. Anna and Serge interpreted his messages
as potentially suicidal. Artiom's mother also reportedly could not locate him; Anna contacted both
parents and spent months fearing that he had died.

This is psychologically important. Anna did not experience a conventional rejection with a known,
living person proceeding elsewhere. She experienced uncertain loss: first romantic possibility,
then withdrawal, then the possibility of death, then discovery that he was alive but did not want
contact. That structure is unusually effective at sustaining fixation because there is neither
mutual continuation nor a clean ending.

### 2024 confrontation

Anna reopened contact seeking a direct account. Artiom said that in 2022 he had deliberately
discarded his whole social circle as part of changing himself and still did not consider the
decision a mistake. He had no wish to restore old friendships. Anna accepted much of the growth
logic but repeatedly asked the narrower question: had he actually felt something for her?

Artiom declined to answer and joked that he enjoyed preserving “the veil of mystery.” He then
described the summer as accidental and “three walks.” Anna supplied a detailed chronology and said
the minimisation induced derealisation. Artiom admitted that much of the period was missing from
his memory and eventually acknowledged that it was not three days.

### 2025 letter and delayed analytical answer

Anna's April letter is an attempt at total testimony. She admits that her repeated attempts to
reach him crossed reasonable boundaries, but asks why he did not let her speak and say goodbye.
She describes depression, disrupted sleep, psychiatric treatment, drinking, smoking, failed
dating, recurring memories, and years of waiting. She calls him a possible soulmate while
recognising that she was not his. She also gives him a playlist intended to communicate what prose
could not.

Artiom did not answer in the thread until almost three months later. He sent three analytical Word
documents, including a psychological architecture of Anna. The related draft material converts
the episode into technical language about “subject lines,” “resource value,” “configuration,” and
“ontological hygiene.” That language may accurately capture his absence of continuing desire, but
it also functions as emotional insulation: it turns a person asking for recognition into an object
of analysis. He gave her an explanation, but not the kind of human acknowledgement she had asked
for.

## Anna as a person

Anna appears verbally gifted, funny, observant and capable of considerable self-disclosure. She
can describe chronology and interior experience with unusual specificity. Even while hurt, she
uses humour, asks permission, accepts that Artiom may not reciprocate, and explicitly takes
responsibility for her present feelings. She rebuilt parts of her life: university, work teaching
Slovak, travel, psychiatric care, abstaining from alcohol and smoking, and new friendships.

Her vulnerability is romantic totalisation. She turned a short, intense period into the organising
myth of several years. She treated the strength of her experience as evidence of unique mutual
meaning, waited for a return despite contrary evidence, compared later men with Artiom, and sought
closure from the person least inclined to provide it. Her admission that she crossed boundaries
should be taken seriously. Her depression cannot be attributed to Artiom alone: emigration,
isolation, bereavement, pre-existing distress and her own repetitive attachment process were also
present.

This does not make her delusional or manipulative. Her factual case that something emotionally and
physically significant occurred is stronger than Artiom's initial minimisation. Her larger belief
that it represented soulmate-level reciprocity is an interpretation the evidence cannot confirm.

## Artiom's role

Artiom did not owe Anna a relationship, continued friendship, travel to Slovakia, or management of
her mental health. Ending an unsuitable attachment was legitimate, and Anna's continued pursuit
could reasonably feel invasive.

He did owe clarity once he chose to engage. The most harmful behaviours visible in the record were:

1. offering romantic and physical cues without defining their meaning;
2. withdrawing through disappearance rather than a clear ending;
3. refusing the exact answer needed to disambiguate the episode while saying he enjoyed the
   mystery;
4. minimising weeks of shared experience to “three walks” despite incomplete memory;
5. meeting suffering with abstraction, jokes and comparative arguments about how much further
   other people go; and
6. eventually answering an intimate letter with a psychological dossier about its author.

There is no evidence that the original summer was a calculated cruel prank. “Rebound” is the more
plausible account. The cruelty lies less in the original attraction than in the later handling of
its consequences.

## Developmental meaning

Anna shows an early version of a recurring Artiom pattern: generate unusually concentrated
intimacy, experience the other person's resulting claim as a threat to autonomy, withdraw, and
then convert the moral problem into a structural or analytical one. The 2025 dossier is a peak
example of using sophisticated language to make detachment feel principled and inevitable.

She also complicates the later belief that women primarily exercise sexual or emotional power over
men. In this episode Artiom held the scarce resource: attention, explanation and recognition. Anna
was the pursuer and he controlled access. The archive therefore contains both configurations of
gendered power, not one universal direction.

## Relationship category

Anna is not best classified as an ex-girlfriend or a current close friend. She was:

- a childhood friend;
- a brief mutual romantic possibility during a rebound;
- an unconsummated and undefined attachment;
- a life-defining lost love in her own biography; and
- a minor but morally revealing attachment in Artiom's biography.

The tragedy is not that one account is true and the other false. The same summer genuinely had
radically different sizes in two minds. The preventable harm came from leaving that asymmetry
unnamed for years.
