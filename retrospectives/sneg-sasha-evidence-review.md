# Sneg (Sasha): evidence review

**Likely relationship period:** before June–August 2022  
**Retained aftermath:** August 2022–October 2023  
**Prepared:** 20 July 2026

## Evidence boundary

The Discord export starts on 11 August 2022, after most or all of the relationship itself.
It preserves only the operator's outgoing messages. No direct message from Sasha survives in
the canonical corpus, and the decisive September 2023 disclosure from Allen is represented
only by the operator's replies to Allen's missing incoming messages.

This report can reconstruct the outline and aftermath, but not reproduce the original
relationship or independently verify every allegation.

Sneg/Sasha is also **not** Snegovik/Sergey, the male participant represented by the tree and
snowman emoji in 2025 group-chat material.

## Short answer

Sasha appears to have entered an online romantic relationship with the operator while also
maintaining or pursuing other attachments. She then withdrew and ignored him for roughly a
month and a half, including after he sent her a birthday greeting, without giving him enough
information to understand what had happened. More than a year later, Allen described his own
March involvement with her. The operator's reactions indicate that Allen's account confirmed
longstanding suspicions: Sasha had a long-term boyfriend, had been involved with Allen and
apparently other men, had wanted a threesome involving Allen, and had regarded Allen—not the
operator—as her primary object of desire. The operator concluded that he had been the
available, inexperienced substitute.

Unlike the Yana case, the surviving Sneg trail points toward a real deception/infidelity
problem rather than merely incompatible conflict styles. But the exact agreements are absent:
the corpus does not establish what Sasha told the operator about her boyfriend, whether the
relationship was explicitly exclusive, or which details were firsthand rather than Allen's
interpretation.

## Chronology

### 1. The relationship and rupture — before the export boundary

The original romantic exchange is missing. Later remarks establish that the operator regarded
what happened with Sasha as an internet relationship. In September 2022 he compared a new
flirtation to “a second Sneg,” said that another Sneg was the last thing he wanted, and said he
deeply regretted the earlier experience. In June 2023 he reminded a friend that after Sneg he
had sworn not to date online again.

The September 2023 reconstruction supplies the likely ending: Sasha ignored him for about a
month and a half, from June to August, and did not answer even when he congratulated her on her
birthday. He spent more than a year trying to understand the event.

What she did with high confidence: ceased reciprocal communication and left the relationship
ambiguous rather than giving him a clear ending. “Ghosting” is a reasonable description if
his dates are accurate.

### 2. Shared-community aftermath — August to December 2022

They remained in the same WoW social environment. An older chat extract records that on 21
May Sasha had not supported the “rats” during a guild conflict. On 30 August the operator
discussed Sasha and others moving together to another guild. In the same exchange he said she
had farmed herbs for him and that he had given her everything he earned, evidence that the
relationship had contained real mutual in-game investment rather than being wholly invented
or one-directional.

On 12 September, a new flirtation frightened him: he was willing to flirt lightly but did not
want “a second Sneg” and said he regretted what had happened intensely. On 31 October he
recalled Sasha rebuking him over a raid tactic. In December he was still reluctant to raid
with her, but told the raid organiser that if no other healer was available, he would accept
her without making a scene.

This shows hurt and avoidance alongside preserved operational restraint. He did not attempt
to expel her from the shared community merely because the relationship had failed.

### 3. Allen's disclosure — 8–9 September 2023

The operator asked Allen what had happened between Allen and Sneg “in March.” Allen's incoming
account is absent, but the operator's replies reveal its major propositions:

- Allen had been Sasha's crush for a long time.
- Allen and Sasha had become sexually involved; the later reference to bruises on her body
  strongly implies physical contact, not merely online flirting.
- Sasha had a boyfriend, and the operator concluded that the boyfriend had been cheated on
  for more than two years.
- Sasha had wanted a threesome involving Allen.
- The operator concluded that Allen was the person she genuinely wanted and that he himself
  had merely been available and inexperienced.
- Allen's disclosure confirmed the operator's earlier suspicions about her conduct.

The operator's immediate response was shock and contamination language: he could barely form
a thought, felt as though he had stepped in excrement and needed to wash, and said the
revelation confirmed his prior model while coming too late to prevent the damage. He said he
had thought about the event for more than a year.

He also told Allen that, if Allen felt guilty about Sasha's boyfriend, that guilt might be
appropriate. At the same time, he said he personally did not care about the boyfriend because
he had never known him. This is an ethically important complication: the retained exchange
does not establish when the operator learned about the boyfriend or what he understood the
relationship rules to be. It cannot safely cast him as an entirely uninvolved victim of the
infidelity toward that boyfriend.

### 4. Retrospective closure — October 2023

On 25 October, while advising a friend through his own relationship uncertainty, the operator
used Sneg as the model. He said that he had spent months winding himself up and doubting what
had happened, but later learned that she had also discarded the other men. His language was
contemptuous, but the functional lesson was clear: ambiguity had prolonged the injury; later
information converted it into a simpler betrayal story.

## What Sasha most likely did

In decreasing order of confidence:

1. **Entered a meaningful online romantic relationship with the operator.**
2. **Withdrew without a clear ending and ignored him for roughly six weeks**, including his
   birthday message to her.
3. **Maintained or pursued overlapping romantic/sexual attachments.** Allen's account and the
   operator's immediate reactions strongly support this, though Allen's original words are
   absent.
4. **Was physically involved with Allen in March while a long-term boyfriend remained in the
   picture.**
5. **Triangulated desire between the operator, Allen, and possibly others.** The threesome
   reference and the operator's “I was merely available” conclusion support this.
6. **Allowed the operator to believe the bond meant more or was more exclusive than her
   behaviour warranted.** Whether this involved an explicit lie is not preserved.
7. **Eventually discarded Allen and/or other men as well**, according to the operator's later
   summary.

## What is not established

- Sasha's own explanation, intentions, and relationship agreements are absent.
- It is not established whether she explicitly promised exclusivity to the operator.
- It is not established when he learned that she had a boyfriend or whether he knowingly
  participated in an affair.
- “She used him” is a plausible interpretation, not a directly preserved admission.
- The allegation of more than two years of cheating is a conclusion from the September
  conversation, not independently corroborated documentation.

## Comparison with Yana

The two relationships should not be collapsed into a single story.

- **Sasha:** ambiguity, silence, overlapping partners, probable infidelity and delayed factual
  discovery. The injury was replacement and epistemic humiliation: the operator did not know
  what relationship he had actually been in.
- **Yana:** high reassurance demands, emotional pressure, conflicting ideas of support,
  escalating arguments, blocking and attempted reconciliation. The injury was perceived
  control and invalidation, not demonstrated sexual betrayal.

Sasha therefore appears more clearly deceptive than Yana on the surviving record. Yana is
better described as an incompatible and mutually escalating partner; Sasha is better
described as an unreliable and probably unfaithful one.

## Developmental significance

Sneg was the earlier wound. By September 2022 the operator already feared repetition and
regretted the relationship intensely. By June 2023, being with Yana explicitly represented a
violation of the protective rule he had made after Sneg: never date online again. Yana's July
rupture did not create distrust from nothing; it landed on an existing template of ambiguity,
replacement and delayed discovery.

The September 2023 Allen disclosure then arrived after the Yana breakup and appeared to
validate the broader distrust model. The operator immediately moved from the individual case
to Schopenhauer's *On Women*. This sequence helps explain the hardening of his gender doctrine:
two different injuries were fused into one general theory. The history explains that theory's
emotional durability, but does not establish its truth about women as a class.
