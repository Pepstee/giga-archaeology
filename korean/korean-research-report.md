# Deep Research Report: Rapid Adult Acquisition of Korean
### Evidence base for an 11-week intensive programme (July–October 2026)

**Method:** 5 parallel research angles (institutional training · learning science · SLA research · polyglot methods · Korean linguistics), ~30 sources fetched and cross-checked, followed by an adversarial verification pass on the 7 most load-bearing claims. Confidence ratings reflect source quality and corroboration. Verification verdicts are noted where a claim was adversarially tested.

---

## 1. How elite institutions do it (DLI, FSI, Middlebury)

**DLI Korean Basic Course: 64 weeks, 6–7 h/day instruction + 2–3 h homework nightly, ≈2,000 class hours.** Class sections of ~6–10 students with teams of native-speaker instructors. ([dliflc.edu](https://www.dliflc.edu/about/)) — HIGH confidence.

**DLI's graduation target for speaking is only ILR 2 (recently raised from 1+).** After ~2,000 hours, highly selected, full-time military students speak at roughly A2–B1 level. This is the single most sobering benchmark for any short-timeline plan. ([DLI "222 by 2022"](https://www.dliflc.edu/getting-to-222-by-2022/), [AUSA](https://www.ausa.org/articles/dlis-language-guidelines)) — HIGH.

**DLI embeds "iso-immersion": 1–3 day overnight scenario simulations** (role-played disputes, phone tasks, interpretation) at a dedicated facility. ([DLI Immersion Language Office](https://www.dliflc.edu/academics/immersion-language-office/)) — HIGH.

**FSI rates Korean Category IV "super-hard": 88 weeks / 2,200 class hours to professional working proficiency (ILR 3)** — and that figure excludes ~15–17 h/week of homework, so true time-on-task approaches 3,500–4,000 hours. Roughly half the training historically happens in-country (Seoul field school). ([state.gov](https://www.state.gov/national-foreign-affairs-training-center/foreign-language-training)) — HIGH for the official figure.

**FSI's own 50-year lessons paper (Jackson & Kaplan 1999):** time-on-task and intensity are the #1 success factor; aptitude is the best individual predictor; **automaticity through drilling is underrated by academic SLA**; maximum efficient class size for hard languages is **4 or fewer**; no single method wins — mature programmes blend approaches. ([sealang.net PDF](http://sealang.net/archives/sla/gurt_1999_07.pdf)) — HIGH. This is the key institutional design document.

**Middlebury's engine is the Language Pledge** — a signed commitment to use only the target language 24/7 for 8 weeks, enforceable by expulsion. Korean School: ~4 h class + 3–5 h homework daily inside the pledge. Claimed gains ≈ one academic year; exit level for beginners ≈ ACTFL Novice High–Intermediate Low (≈ A2). ([middlebury.edu](https://www.middlebury.edu/language-schools/how-it-works/language-pledge)) — HIGH for structure, MEDIUM for gains claims.

### Verified benchmark: what 11 weeks can deliver
**ADVERSARIAL VERDICT — SUPPORTED:** an adult starting from zero, ~5–6 h/day focused study while living in Korea for 11 weeks (~450 h), lands at **ILR 1–1+ / solid A2 speaking: confident survival Korean plus simple social conversation on familiar and rehearsed topics**. No independently assessed counterexample of B1 speaking in Korean in 3 months from zero was found. The honest ceiling for outliers is "high A2 with islands of B1-like performance on rehearsed territory". ~450 focused hours ≈ completing Yonsei/Sogang KLI Levels 1–2 ≈ TOPIK 1–2 range. ([Yonsei KLI](https://www.yskli.com/course.php?mid=E01_01), [ILR descriptors](https://www.govtilr.org/Skills/ILRscale2.htm), [Middlebury Korean levels](https://www.middlebury.edu/language-schools/languages/korean/immersion-program)) 
**Note:** TOPIK has no speaking section — do not use it to measure the goal that matters. Use OPI-style speaking assessments at weeks 0/6/11.

## 2. Learning science (memory, scheduling, load)

**Spaced repetition / FSRS.** The open srs-benchmark (≈350M reviews, ~10k Anki collections): FSRS log-loss 0.291 vs SM-2's 0.354; FSRS-6 predicts better than SM-2 for 99.6% of users. The popular "20–30% fewer reviews at equal retention" figure is **simulation-derived, not a controlled trial**, and the advantage shrinks early on before per-user parameters can be fitted (<~1,000 reviews). Still clearly the best scheduler. ([expertium.github.io benchmark](https://expertium.github.io/Benchmark.html), [srs-benchmark repo](https://github.com/open-spaced-repetition/srs-benchmark)) — VERDICT: SUPPORTED-WITH-CAVEATS.

**Retrieval practice.** Adesope et al. 2017 meta-analysis (272 effects): practice testing g = 0.61 overall, +0.51 vs restudying. Retrieval beats rereading — flashcards, self-testing and forced recall are the correct default mode for everything. ([Adesope et al.](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2a2756b75e3ab15e6bda29203f9be32b90772e60)) — HIGH.

**Sleep consolidates new vocabulary.** Gaskell & Dumay's word-learning studies: newly learned words integrate into the mental lexicon only after sleep (lexical competition effects emerge after a night's sleep, not immediately). Studying vocabulary in the evening before sleep is a cheap win; protect 7.5–8 h sleep as part of the study system, not a competitor to it. ([Dumay & Gaskell line of work](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13423-021-01980-3)) — HIGH for the consolidation effect.

**Habit machinery.** Median time to automaticity for a new daily habit: 66 days (range 18–254) — conveniently, almost exactly this programme's length (Lally et al. 2010). Implementation intentions ("when X, I do Y") improve goal attainment d = 0.65 across 94 tests (Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006). Anchor every study block to a fixed daily trigger. ([Lally 2010](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/ejsp.674), [Gollwitzer & Sheeran meta](https://cancercontrol.cancer.gov/sites/default/files/2020-06/goal_intent_attain.pdf)) — HIGH.

**Daily-hour limits.** **ADVERSARIAL VERDICT — SUPPORTED-WITH-CAVEATS:** the famous "~4 h/day deliberate-practice ceiling" is folklore compression of a correlational violinist study whose headline findings failed direct replication (Macnamara & Maitra 2019). There is no evidenced hard cliff. But the design conclusion survives: FSI/DLI students sustain **7–8 h/day total for months only because intensity is mixed** — classes, homework, immersion — and every documented self-directed failure case (see §4) is an overload failure. Correct framing: concentrate the most effortful work in ~4–6 h; fill remaining hours with lower-intensity immersion. ([Macnamara & Maitra replication](https://royalsocietypublishing.org/doi/10.1098/rsos.190327), [Ericsson rebuttal](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6824411/))

## 3. SLA research: what actually works

**Input is necessary, not sufficient.** Krashen's comprehensible-input idea survives as the volume engine (extensive reading meta-analyses: d ≈ 0.46–0.71), but the strong "input-only, correction useless" claims are contradicted by three decades of meta-analyses. ([Nakanishi 2015](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/tesq.157)) — HIGH.

**Interaction with corrective feedback robustly accelerates acquisition** (Mackey & Goo 2007; Li 2010: d = 0.61 for oral corrective feedback, durable). **Prompts that force self-repair beat recasts** (teacher reformulations) — make the learner produce the corrected form (Lyster & Saito 2010). ([Li 2010](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1467-9922.2010.00561.x), [Lyster & Saito](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/studies-in-second-language-acquisition/article/abs/oral-feedback-in-classroom-sla/4999EE1C8379B2BF026B148EAF373CA1)) — HIGH.

**Explicit grammar instruction helps adults — in small doses.** Norris & Ortega 2000 and Goo et al. 2015 found explicit > implicit; Kang, Sok & Han 2019 (54 studies, N=5,051) shrank the gap and showed the delivery mode matters less than practice. Best reading: brief explicit focus embedded in communicative practice; grammar-first syllabi are not supported. ([Kang et al. 2019](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/1362168818776671)) — HIGH.

**The cautionary tale of "Wes"** (Schmidt 1983): years of naturalistic conversation, superb fluency and pragmatics, near-zero grammatical development. Pure talk without attention to form fossilises. Output must be feedback-looped. ([Schmidt](https://nflrc.hawaii.edu/PDFs/SCHMIDT%20Attention,%20awareness,%20and%20individual%20differences.pdf)) — HIGH.

**Shadowing** improves bottom-up listening (phoneme decoding), with gains concentrated in beginners; improves comprehensibility and fluency of speech but not accent. Proper protocol (Hamada): gist listen → mumble-shadow → synchronised reading → prosody shadow → content shadow; 10–15 min/day on slightly-easy audio; self-record weekly. Frame it as listening training. ([Hamada 2016](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/1362168815597504), [Foote & McDonough 2017](https://www.jbe-platform.com/content/journals/10.1075/jslp.3.1.02foo)) — HIGH.

**Pronunciation training is one of the largest effects in the field** (Lee, Jang & Plonsky 2015: d ≈ 0.8) and adults CAN build new phoneme categories: HVPT — identification drills on minimal pairs across many voices with feedback — yields g ≈ 0.67–0.92, retained and generalising, with perception gains transferring to production. Gains are cheapest early ("window of maximal opportunity"); "pronunciation comes later" contradicts the literature. ([HVPT meta-analysis, SSLA 2025](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/studies-in-second-language-acquisition/article/high-variability-phonetic-training-hvpt-a-metaanalysis-of-l2-perceptual-training-studies/6ABB8C1F32D88D53EA8D05A4565E76F6), [Lee et al. 2015](https://academic.oup.com/applij/article/36/3/345/2422438)) — HIGH.

**Vocabulary.** Deliberate flashcard learning is the fastest way to seed form-meaning links, and **word cards with L1 translations work as well as or better than contextual/sentence formats for initial learning** (Laufer & Shmueli 1997; Webb 2007 found no context advantage). ADVERSARIAL VERDICT — SUPPORTED, with scope limit: word cards build the first layer only; collocation, register and grammar knowledge still come from massive input afterwards. Incidental learning from input is real but slow (~8–12 encounters per word). ([Laufer & Shmueli](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/003368829702800106), [Webb 2007](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1362168806072463), [Uchihara et al. 2019](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/lang.12343)) — HIGH.

**Coverage targets.** In English, ~3,000 word families ≈ 95% coverage of conversation, and 95% is adequate for listening. **ADVERSARIAL VERDICT — CONTESTED for Korean:** no Korean-specific replication exists; Korean frequency research counts eojeol/lemmas, not word families, and grammatical morphemes change the calculus. Treat "~3,000 lemmas + strong ending-recognition" as a rough analogue, not an established equivalence. ([Nation 2006](https://www.lextutor.ca/cover/papers/nation_2006.pdf), [van Zeeland & Schmitt](https://academic.oup.com/applij/article-abstract/34/4/457/199564)) 

**Fluency is trained, not awaited.** The 4/3/2 technique (same talk to new listeners in 4, then 3, then 2 minutes) reliably raises speech rate; exact task repetition improves accuracy and complexity; explicitly learned formulaic chunks make speech sound fluent because they cut online processing load. ([Boers et al. 2006](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1191/1362168806lr195oa), [Thai & Boers](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/284402076_A_Reappraisal_of_the_432_Activity)) — HIGH.

**Nation's four strands** — the best available macro-blueprint: ~25% each of meaning-focused input, meaning-focused output, language-focused learning (vocab/grammar/pronunciation study), and fluency development. ([Nation 2007](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.2167/illt039.0)) — HIGH.

## 4. Polyglots and immersion communities: signal vs survivorship

**Where all successful methods agree** (the signal): enormous daily target-language contact (4–16 h); input as the bulk of the diet; SRS for vocabulary; content you actually enjoy (the binding constraint is quitting); grammar as light reference; and **killing English in the environment** (Young's no-English pact = AJATT's environment conversion = Middlebury's pledge).

**Where they conflict, the resolution is now settled:** Refold itself has officially retreated from strict input-before-output ("waiting isn't necessarily most efficient... the guide is an example path, not a bible"). With an 11-week deadline, early feedback-rich speaking is mandatory; the "silent period" is a luxury of 5-year horizons. ([Refold's own concession](https://refold.la/blog/when-are-you-ready-to-output-2/)) — HIGH.

**Documented failure modes are all overload or foundation failures, never underwork:**
- Scott Young's "Year Without English" succeeded in Spain/Brazil/China but **Korean was the failure case** — diagnosis: no pre-arrival foundation, cumulative burnout, and Koreans switching to English collapsing the no-English rule. ([Young's retrospective](https://www.scotthyoung.com/blog/2014/09/01/tywe-review/), [Korean update](https://www.scotthyoung.com/blog/2014/09/10/korean-final-update/)) — HIGH; the single most relevant case study for this project.
- A 5 h/day italki tutoring experiment left the learner "wrecked" by session 4–5; practical live-conversation ceiling ≈ 2–3 h/day. ([Mezzoguild](https://www.mezzoguild.com/italki-experiment-results/)) — MEDIUM.
- AJATT/Refold burnout reports are common; Matt vs Japan himself describes depression and social isolation during his maximalist phase. — MEDIUM-HIGH.
- Benny Lewis's public 3-month missions fell short of their targets (Mandarin B1-ish after ~5 months); what survives scrutiny is that speaking early kills speaking anxiety, not the timelines. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benny_Lewis)) — HIGH.

**Worth stealing:** Arguelles' walking shadowing (loud, simultaneous, brisk — lab-corroborated); Wyner's pronunciation-first sequencing and self-made cards (generation effect); Kaufmann's reading-while-listening loop; measured italki data showing tutoring hours convert to oral gains unusually efficiently at beginner level ([Vesselinov 2018](https://www.gettingsmart.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/01/italki2018FinalReport.pdf)).

## 5. Korean-specific findings (highest leverage)

**The stop contrast is about pitch now.** In modern Seoul Korean the old VOT distinction between lenis (ㄱ) and aspirated (ㅋ) stops has merged; **the primary cue is the pitch of the following vowel — low = lenis (가), high = aspirated (카) or tense (까)** — active tonogenesis, heavily replicated, strongest in exactly the younger Seoul speakers you'll talk to. ADVERSARIAL VERDICT — SUPPORTED (caveat: applies phrase-initially; between vowels lenis stops voice instead: 고기 ≈ "kogi→gogi"). Learners taught to attend to pitch acquire the contrast; textbooks almost never mention this. ([Kang 2014, J. Phonetics](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0095447014000357), [Bang et al. 2018](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0095447017302188)) — HIGH.
- Your L1 bonus: tense consonants (ㄲㄸㅃㅆㅉ) ≈ Russian/Romanian unaspirated stops; ㅡ ≈ Russian ы / Romanian î; Romanian ă ≈ ㅓ. The pair to drill hard: **ㅓ vs ㅗ**, and lenis-vs-aspirated by pitch.

**Batchim + 4 sound-change rules explain "why can't I hear words I know":** final stops are unreleased; liaison (음악→으-막), nasalisation (십만→심만), tensification (학교→학꾜), ㅎ-weakening/aspiration (좋아요→조아요, 좋다→조타). Learn these 4 explicitly (they matter more for listening than speaking), then automatise via audio. Billy Go's free Hangul series parts 11–14 covers exactly this. ([GoBillyKorean](https://gobillykorean.com/learn-hangul-part-11-sound-change-rules-names-letters/)) — HIGH.

**Speech levels: default 100% to 해요체.** Polite for strangers, elders, shops, classmates — the practitioner consensus is unambiguous, and modern curricula teach it first (older textbooks over-teach stiff 합니다체). ADVERSARIAL VERDICT — SUPPORTED-WITH-CAVEATS: you must *recognise* 합쇼체 from day 1 (announcements, staff, news), and if your October scenario involves formal settings (interviews, officials), memorise a handful of -습니다 frames. The real rudeness risk is uninvited 반말, not 해요체. Honorific starter kit: -(으)시- recognition + 드시다/계시다/주무시다 + 씨 after given name (never surname alone). ([90DayKorean](https://www.90daykorean.com/korean-speech-levels/), [Korean honorifics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_honorifics)) — HIGH.

**Grammar geometry.** Korean conjugation has NO person/number agreement and its six "irregular" verb classes are rule-governed — morphology is not the threat. The threat is real-time SOV clause assembly: drill whole verb-final patterns aloud until automatic. For 은/는 vs 이/가 use the 70% heuristic (topic/contrast vs new information/identification) and move on — full mastery is exposure-driven and intermediate-level; do not stall on it. ([HowToStudyKorean](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/unit1/unit-1-lessons-1-8/unit-1-lesson-2/)) — HIGH.

**Vocabulary multipliers.** Both number systems early, split by function (Sino-Korean: dates/money/minutes/phone; native: hours/age/counting), with 6 counters covering most daily life (개 명 마리 시 분 살 + 원). Sino-Korean ≈ 57% of the lexicon: learning ~50 high-yield Hanja *roots as morphemes* (학 = study → 학생/학교/대학/과학...) unlocks thousands of words — no character-writing needed. Konglish loanwords are hundreds of freebies once re-pronounced Korean-style (커피, 버스), with a short false-friend list (핸드폰 = mobile). ([Sino-Korean vocabulary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sino-Korean_vocabulary), [Monash numbers guide](https://www.monash.edu/arts/languages-literatures-cultures-linguistics/korean-studies-research-hub/research/murksrh-language-lab/topic-1-how-to-know-when-to-use-pure-korean-vs-sino-korean-numbers)) — HIGH.

**Register gap.** Spoken Korean systematically contracts (것을→걸, 이것이→이게, -지요→-죠, 그런데→근데); textbooks under-teach this, K-dramas over-teach 반말 and slang. Method: produce from textbook patterns, ear-train with media. ([Practice Korean contractions](https://practice-korean.com/grammar/contractions/)) — HIGH.

## 6. Synthesis: the 12 design principles the system is built on

1. **Set the target honestly:** solid A2 speaking (ILR 1/1+) — survival + real social conversation on familiar ground. Stretch goal: high-A2 with B1 islands on rehearsed territory. (Verified against every institutional benchmark.)
2. **Copy elite intensity structure, not totals:** ~4–6 h deliberate work + 2–3 h light immersion daily, 6 days/week, mixed intensity — the FSI/DLI pattern.
3. **Time-on-task is the #1 predictor** (FSI's own conclusion). Protect the daily volume above all else.
4. **Nation's four strands as the skeleton:** ≈25% each input / output / language-focused study / fluency work.
5. **Pronunciation and ear first** (HVPT minimal pairs by pitch cue, weeks 1–3) — largest effect sizes in the field, cheapest early, prevents fossilised errors.
6. **Deliberate vocabulary is the fastest lever:** Anki+FSRS word cards with L1 translations, frequency-ordered, evening sessions before sleep; input consolidates what cards seed.
7. **Speak early but feedback-looped** (the Wes rule): daily tutoring with prompts-not-recasts, errors → cards. Both the input-purist and talk-only extremes are refuted.
8. **Grammar as brief explicit focus** embedded in use — never grammar-first, never grammar-never.
9. **Train fluency mechanically:** 4/3/2, task repetition, formulaic chunk drilling — automaticity is FSI's underrated weapon.
10. **Kill English structurally, not by willpower:** pledge blocks, environment conversion, scripts for when Koreans switch to English (the exact failure that sank Scott Young's Korea attempt).
11. **Burnout is the #1 documented failure mode of self-directed intensive learners.** Periodise: hard/easy days, one rest evening, deload weeks, live-conversation capped ~2–3 h/day.
12. **Measure speaking, not TOPIK:** OPI-style assessments at weeks 0/6/11 + weekly recorded monologue; mid-course re-scope is planned, not a failure.
