# Korean by October — The Master Plan

**Start:** 16 July 2026 · **Deadline:** 1 October 2026 · **Runway: 77 days (11 weeks)**
**Starting point:** absolute zero · **Location advantage:** you live in Korea
**Target:** functional conversational Korean (roughly A2+/low B1 speaking, TOPIK 2 territory) — able to hold real conversations about daily life, yourself, plans, opinions on familiar topics, and handle any everyday situation in Korean.

That target is ambitious but achievable at your time budget. Korean is an FSI Category IV language, but "conversational" is a fraction of "professionally proficient". At ~400–450 focused hours plus living immersed in-country, this is done regularly by motivated learners. What kills most people is not lack of time — it is inconsistency and burnout. This plan is built around both.

---

## 1. The science this plan is built on

Every element below has strong evidence behind it. No apps-as-games, no passive-only listening, no "learn while you sleep" nonsense.

**Comprehensible input (Krashen; supported by decades of SLA research).** You acquire language by understanding messages slightly above your level ("i+1"). The bulk of your hours will be listening/reading to content you mostly understand — not memorising rules.

**Retrieval practice and spaced repetition (Ebbinghaus; Karpicke & Roediger's testing effect).** Actively recalling beats rereading by a wide margin. Anki with the FSRS algorithm (enabled in modern Anki) achieves the same retention with 20–30% fewer reviews than the old SM-2 scheduler.

**The output hypothesis and interaction (Swain; Long).** Speaking forces you to notice gaps that input alone hides. Interaction with correction is where speaking ability is actually built. You speak from week 1, badly, on purpose.

**Deliberate practice (Ericsson).** Progress comes from working at the edge of your ability on identified weaknesses with feedback — not from comfortable repetition. Tutors and I exist to give you that feedback loop.

**Frequency-first vocabulary (Zipf's law).** The most frequent ~1,000 Korean words cover the large majority of everyday speech; ~2,000 gets you conversational coverage. You learn words in frequency order, then switch to mining words from your own immersion.

**Shadowing and chorusing (Arguelles).** Repeating speech aloud simultaneously or immediately after a native speaker builds pronunciation, prosody and listening parsing far faster than silent study.

**Consolidation.** Sleep, spacing study across the day, and exercise all measurably improve retention. Cramming one 10-hour block is worse than the same hours distributed.

**The study/immersion ratio.** Current best practice (Refold and similar immersion methodologies): cap deliberate "study" (grammar, Anki) at ~25–30% of total time; the rest is input and interaction. Study is the scaffold; immersion is the building.

## 3. Phase plan

### Phase 0 — Hangul (Days 1–3)
Hangul is a featural alphabet learnable in hours, not weeks.
- Learn all consonants/vowels + batchim (final consonant) rules. Ryan Estrada's "Learn to read Korean in 15 minutes" mnemonics + writing every character by hand.
- Read street signs, menus, shop names around you — you live in the world's best Hangul flashcard deck.
- **Exit test:** read any Korean word aloud slowly but correctly, write your name and address.

### Phase 1 — Foundation (Weeks 1–3)
- **Grammar:** TTMIK Levels 1–3 (or *Korean Grammar in Use: Beginning*), 1–2 lessons/day. Understand, don't master — mastery comes from seeing patterns in input.
- **Vocab:** Anki, frequency deck (e.g. Evita's Korean Grammar Sentences / a top-2k frequency deck), 25–30 new cards/day, FSRS on.
- **Listening:** beginner comprehensible input on YouTube (e.g. Comprehensible Korean, Iyagi beginner) 1–2 h/day.
- **Speaking:** from Week 2, daily 30–60 min tutor lesson (italki) or in-person class. Survival script drills: ordering, shopping, taxi, "I'm learning Korean, please speak slowly."
- **Real-world missions (daily, you're in Korea):** order food in Korean only; ask a price; ask directions even when you know the way.
- **Exit test:** self-introduction of 60+ seconds; understand slow clear speech on daily topics; ~700 words.

### Phase 2 — Conversation engine (Weeks 4–7)
- **Grammar:** TTMIK Levels 4–6; start *Korean Grammar in Use* as reference, not textbook.
- **Vocab:** switch from frequency deck to **sentence mining** — every unknown word you meet in immersion that feels useful becomes an Anki card with its sentence. Tools: Language Reactor (Netflix/YouTube dual subtitles) or Migaku.
- **Input:** 2–3 h/day: easy K-dramas/variety with Korean subs, comprehensible-input podcasts, webtoons (Naver 웹툰, start with children's/daily-life genres).
- **Shadowing:** 20–30 min/day on short native clips; record yourself weekly and compare.
- **Speaking:** 1 h/day minimum — tutor + language exchange (HelloTalk/Tandem meet-ups; Seoul Global Center and Global Village Centres run free classes and exchanges).
- **Writing:** daily 5–10 sentence journal; corrections from tutor or me.
- **Exit test:** 10-minute unscripted conversation about your day/week with a patient native speaker; ~1,500 words.

### Phase 3 — Fluency pressure (Weeks 8–11)
- **Korean-only blocks:** start with Korean-only afternoons, build to 2 full Korean-only days/week (phone OS in Korean, self-talk in Korean, no English media).
- **Speaking:** 1–1.5 h/day; push into opinions, stories, hypotheticals, your reasons for staying in Korea — the actual October conversations. Rehearse them explicitly.
- **Input:** native-level content with Korean subs; graduate off subtitles for familiar shows.
- **Deliberate repair:** keep a "gap log" of everything you failed to say; drill those exact patterns next day.
- **Week 11:** simulate October. Whatever conversation determines your return — visa interview, partner's family, employer — we script, drill and stress-test it repeatedly.
- **Exit test:** 20+ minute spontaneous conversation; understand ~70% of clear native speech on familiar topics.

## 5. Rules (non-negotiable)

1. **Anki every single day.** Miss a day and the debt compounds. It is the one thing you do even on rest days (reviews only).
2. **No romanisation after Day 3.** It cripples pronunciation and reading speed.
3. **Speak from Week 2, badly.** Errors are the mechanism, not the enemy. You are not allowed to "wait until ready".
4. **English is a tool, not a habitat.** Every service interaction in Korea happens in Korean, even when the other person switches to English — politely continue in Korean.
5. **Input must be comprehensible.** If you understand <50%, the content is too hard — go easier. Struggling through noise is not immersion.
6. **Track everything.** Daily log: hours, new cards, what you failed to say. What gets measured gets managed.
7. **One rest evening per week and one light day per week.** Not optional — see below.

## 6. Burnout management (your real constraint)

You said it yourself: time is infinite, energy is not. Treat this like athletic periodisation.

- **Cap active study at 5–7 h/day.** Research on attention and skill acquisition is clear that quality collapses beyond this; 10 focused hours daily is a two-week burnout plan. Fill remaining enthusiasm with *passive* Korean (music, background TV, Korean-speaking social time) which costs little willpower.
- **Hard/easy alternation:** roughly 3 standard days → 1 lighter day (~60% load, more fun input, no grammar).
- **Weekly:** one evening completely off. Guilt-free, English allowed.
- **Every 4th week is a deload week:** ~70% volume, consolidation over new material. Weeks 4 and 8.
- **Vary modalities within a day** — the schedule above alternates reading/listening/speaking/writing precisely so fatigue in one channel doesn't end the day.
- **Motivation hygiene:** attach study to fixed anchors (Anki with morning coffee), make immersion content you *actually enjoy* the reward, and track a visible streak. If you feel dread two days running, we cut volume 30% for three days — that instruction comes from me, so you don't have to negotiate with yourself.
