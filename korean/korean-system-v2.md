# KOREAN OPERATING SYSTEM v2.0
### Self-contained. Runs with or without any AI assistant. Supersedes v1.

**Operator:** Artiom · **Start:** 16 July 2026 · **Deadline:** 1 October 2026 · **Runway:** 77 days
**Mission:** functional conversational Korean before leaving Korea in October.
**Verified target (see korean-research-report.md):** solid **A2 speaking (ILR 1/1+)** — confident survival Korean + real social conversation on familiar topics. Stretch: high-A2 with B1-level performance on *rehearsed* territory (your October conversations will be rehearsed territory — that is the exploit).

This document is the single source of truth. If the AI assistant's memory is wiped, your tools break, or motivation dies, everything needed to continue is in here (§10 is the disaster-recovery procedure).

## 1. Architecture overview

The system has four load-bearing subsystems, mapped to Nation's four strands (~25% of time each):

| Subsystem | Function | Daily vehicle |
|---|---|---|
| INPUT | comprehension engine | graded listening/reading, media |
| OUTPUT | speaking with feedback | tutor, exchanges, missions, journal |
| STUDY | vocabulary, grammar, pronunciation | Anki, TTMIK, HVPT drills |
| FLUENCY | automaticity | shadowing, 4/3/2, chunk drills, task repetition |

Failure of any one subsystem degrades the others. The weekly review (§8) checks all four.

**Design constraints (evidence-verified):**
- Time-on-task is the #1 predictor of outcome. Protect daily volume above all else.
- Burnout is the #1 documented failure mode of self-directed intensive learners. Deliberate work capped at 4–6 h/day; total Korean contact 7–8 h is fine when intensity is mixed.
- Every speaking hour must have a feedback loop (the "Wes rule": talk without form-focus fossilises).
- Kill English structurally, not with willpower.

## 2. Phase map

### PHASE 0 — Sound system + Hangul (Days 1–4)
Pronunciation first (largest effect sizes in SLA; cheapest now, fossilisation-proofing).
- Learn Hangul completely: all jamo + syllable blocks. Resource: Billy Go's Hangul series (free, YouTube) or TTMIK Hangul course.
- **The pitch secret (textbooks don't teach this):** in modern Seoul Korean, lenis vs aspirated stops (가 vs 카) are distinguished mainly by the PITCH of the following vowel — lenis starts LOW, aspirated and tense start HIGH. Train your ear on pitch, not puff-of-air. (Phrase-initial only; between vowels ㄱ sounds like "g".)
- Your freebies: tense sounds ㄲㄸㅃㅆㅉ ≈ Russian/Romanian unaspirated stops; ㅡ ≈ ы/î; ă ≈ ㅓ. Drill hard: **ㅓ vs ㅗ**.
- Learn the 4 sound-change rules for LISTENING: liaison (음악→으막), nasalisation (십만→심만), tensification (학교→학꾜), ㅎ-rules (좋아요→조아요). Billy Go parts 11–14.
- Batchim: final stops are NEVER released. Say 밥 like stopping mid-word.
- **Exit test:** read any Korean word aloud; pass 20/25 on a minimal-pair listening quiz (가/카/까 sets); write name + address.

### PHASE 1 — Foundation (Weeks 1–3)
- Grammar: TTMIK Levels 1–3, 1–2 lessons/day, each lesson → 5 self-written sentences spoken aloud.
- Vocab: Anki 20–25 new words/day, frequency-ordered (see §5 for card format).
- HVPT drill: 10 min/day minimal-pair identification (multiple speakers, immediate feedback) on 가/카/까, 다/타/따, 바/파/빠, ㅓ/ㅗ.
- Listening: 1–1.5 h/day beginner comprehensible input (YouTube: Comprehensible Korean; TTMIK Iyagi beginner).
- Speaking: from Day 8, daily 30–60 min tutor session (§6 has the tutor briefing).
- Daily real-world mission (§7).
- Numbers: both systems, split by function (Sino: dates/money/minutes; native: hours/age/counting) + 6 counters: 개 명 마리 시 분 살.
- **Exit test:** 60-second self-introduction; order a full meal + handle a follow-up question; ~600 words in Anki with >85% young retention.

### PHASE 2 — Conversation engine (Weeks 4–7)
- Grammar: TTMIK Levels 4–6; *Korean Grammar in Use* as lookup reference only.
- Vocab: continue frequency cards + start mining words from your own conversations and media (tutor corrections and failed-to-say items get priority as cards).
- Input: 2–3 h/day — easy K-content with Korean subs (Language Reactor on Netflix/YouTube), webtoons (Naver 웹툰 daily-life genres). Produce from textbook register; ear-train with media (dramas over-teach 반말).
- Shadowing: 15 min/day, Hamada protocol (§4), walking, out loud.
- Fluency: 4/3/2 twice a week (§4); weekly recorded 2-minute monologue (keep every recording — this is your progress instrument).
- Speaking: 1–1.5 h/day total: tutor + language exchange (HelloTalk/Tandem apps; in-person 언어교환 meet-ups).
- Writing: nightly 5–10 sentence journal → corrections → error cards.
- Korean-only afternoons begin Week 6 (2×/week).
- **Exit test:** 10-minute unscripted conversation about your week; ~1,500 words; understand a webtoon page without dictionary at >80%.

### PHASE 3 — Fluency pressure + October rehearsal (Weeks 8–11)
- Korean-only days: 2/week, building to 3 (phone OS in Korean, self-talk in Korean).
- Speaking: 1.5 h/day; deliberately push opinions, stories, hypotheticals, plans — the actual content of October conversations.
- **Gap log discipline:** every single thing you fail to say gets written down and drilled within 24 h.
- **October simulation:** define the exact conversations your return depends on (interview? family? officials? employer?). Script them, build the vocabulary, then drill under pressure: tutor plays the counterpart cold, no warnings, multiple runs (task repetition measurably raises accuracy + complexity). If formal settings are involved, add 10 memorised -습니다 frames.
- Input: native content, gradually dropping subtitles on familiar shows.
- **Exit test:** 20-minute spontaneous conversation; October scenarios pass repeatedly without English fallback.

## 4. Protocols (exact procedures)

**HVPT minimal-pair drill (10 min):** hear a syllable → identify which of 2–3 candidates it was → immediate feedback → next. Multiple different speakers, randomised. Focus pairs: lenis/aspirated (by pitch!), ㅓ/ㅗ. Sources: any minimal-pair trainer, tutor-made recordings, or AI-generated audio quizzes.

**Shadowing (Hamada protocol, 15 min):** (1) listen once for gist → (2) mumble-shadow along → (3) shadow while reading the transcript → (4) shadow prosody without transcript, copying rhythm/pitch → (5) shadow for meaning. Material: SLIGHTLY BELOW your level, 30–60 s clips. Out loud, walking, briskly. Record yourself every Sunday; compare monthly.

**4/3/2 fluency drill (2×/week):** pick a topic you can handle. Deliver it in 4 minutes. Same talk again in 3. Again in 2. To a tutor, exchange partner, or voice recorder. The time pressure + repetition builds automatic retrieval.

**Journal loop (nightly):** write 5–10 sentences about the day → get corrections (tutor or AI) → for each error, note the pattern → make 1–3 cards → next day, re-say the corrected sentences aloud.

**Chunk drilling:** maintain a list of formulaic sequences (whole phrases: 그런 것 같아요, 말씀 좀 묻겠는데요, ~(으)ㄹ 수 있어요?). Drill them as wholes until they fire without assembly. Chunks are what make you SOUND fluent — they cut processing load.

**Mission debrief:** after each real-world mission, note (a) what you managed, (b) the exact sentence you wished you could say → gap log → cards.

## 5. Anki configuration

- Scheduler: **FSRS enabled**, desired retention 0.90; run "Optimize" monthly once >1,000 reviews.
- New cards: 20–25/day standard, 10/day on light days, 0 during deload weeks (reviews continue).
- Card format for vocabulary: **Korean word → L1 meaning** and reverse; word cards with translations are research-validated for initial learning — do NOT feel guilty about "no context". Add one example sentence on the back, audio on the front where possible.
- Error cards (from journal/tutor/gap log): front = the situation/English intent, back = the correct Korean sentence. Say it aloud before flipping.
- Never miss reviews. Reviews before new cards. Evening session sits before sleep on purpose.
- Expected total by 1 Oct at this rate: ~1,800–2,300 words + several hundred chunks/patterns. (The English "3,000 families = 95% of speech" figure is not Korean-validated — treat coverage as directional, and let conversation needs drive mining.)

## 6. Tutor briefing (hand this to every tutor/partner)

> 1. Speak Korean at natural-but-clear speed; slow down rather than switch to English.
> 2. When I make an error, do NOT just repeat the correct form — prompt me to fix it myself ("다시?", puzzled look, or point at the error). Only explain if I fail twice. (Prompts beat recasts — meta-analytic finding.)
> 3. Default register: 해요체. Correct me if I drift into 반말.
> 4. Each session: ~10 min warm-up on yesterday's topics (task repetition), ~30 min new ground, ~10 min drilling my recent gap-log items.
> 5. End by sending me my top 5 errors as text.
- Volume: 1 h/day (Phases 1–2) → 1.5 h/day (Phase 3). Cap live conversation at ≤2–3 h/day — documented burnout ceiling.
- Rotate 2–3 tutors for voice variety (aids perception training).
- Cheap/free alternatives while in Korea: KIIP classes (socinet.go.kr — free, and levels earn visa/residency points: check eligibility NOW, it may directly affect October), Seoul Global Center / Global Village Centres free classes, university language exchanges, 언어교환 meet-ups.

## 7. The anti-English protocols

English collapse is what sank the best-documented 3-month Korea attempt (Scott Young's). Structural countermeasures:

- **The switch script.** When a Korean switches to English, smile and say: "저 한국어 연습해야 돼요. 한국어로 해도 돼요?" (I need to practise Korean — can we do it in Korean?). If they persist, answer their English in Korean. Drill this phrase until reflexive — it is your single most important sentence.
- **Environment conversion (Week 1):** phone OS → Korean; music/podcasts → Korean; social feeds → Korean-learning content; labels on objects at home.
- **Pledge blocks:** formal, scheduled, escalating — Korean-only afternoons (Week 6) → full days (Phase 3). Treat like Middlebury's pledge: a rule, not a mood.
- **Missions (daily, escalating):** Week 1: order/pay/greet. Week 3: ask for recommendations, make small talk with staff. Week 5: phone call (reservation). Week 7: a bureaucratic errand in Korean. Week 9+: strangers, longer conversations, favours.

## 8. Instrumentation (what gets measured)

**Daily log (2 min, one line):** date · active hours · Anki done Y/N + new cards · speaking minutes · mission done · energy score 1–5 · one thing I couldn't say.

**Weekly review (Sundays, 20 min):**
1. Four strands balanced this week? (input/output/study/fluency)
2. Record the 2-minute monologue. Listen to last week's.
3. Gap log → next week's drill list.
4. Energy trend: two consecutive days ≤2 → mandatory 30% volume cut for 3 days (this rule decides, not you).
5. Milestone check (§9). Slipping >1 week → re-scope: cut new vocab, increase speaking. Conversational ability is the goal, not completion of materials.

**Speaking assessments (weeks 0, 6, 11):** 15-minute recorded conversation with a tutor told to probe: self-intro → daily life → past events → opinions → October scenario. Rate against the can-do lists in §9. Week 0 = baseline (it will be near-zero; that's the point of the recording).

## 10. Disaster recovery (if AI memory is lost / tools break)

**The system state lives in three places you control:**
1. This file + korean-research-report.md (the why behind every rule)
2. Your Anki collection (the entire vocabulary/error state — back it up weekly: File → Export → .colpkg → cloud)
3. Your daily log + weekly monologue recordings (progress instrumentation)

**To rebootstrap any AI assistant from zero, paste this:**
> I am learning Korean intensively until 1 October 2026, in Korea, following a fixed system document (korean-system-v2.md — attached). Current phase: [X]. Current word count: [X]. My role for you: (1) daily practice partner at my level in 해요체, (2) journal corrector — correct errors by prompting me to self-repair first, then explain patterns, (3) drill generator for my gap log, (4) weekly reviewer against the system's §8. Do not redesign the system; operate it.

**If a tool dies:** Anki dies → paper Leitner boxes (5 boxes, daily/2/4/8/16-day schedule). Tutor unavailable → HelloTalk voice calls + language exchange meet-ups. Netflix/Language Reactor dies → YouTube + Korean subtitles. Nothing about this system depends on any single product.

**If motivation dies:** execute the §3 non-negotiable list only (Anki + 30 min + 2-line journal) for up to 5 days. That preserves the streak and the state. Then run a weekly review and re-scope. Quitting entirely is the only unrecoverable failure; the median time to habit automaticity is ~66 days — day 40 feeling mechanical is the system working, not failing.
