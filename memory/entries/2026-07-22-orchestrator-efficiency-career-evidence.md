The orchestrator-v3/JAA efficiency failure is itself valuable career evidence **if the next
phase demonstrates a measured improvement without weakening correctness**. The strongest
positioning is not merely “the orchestrator built JAA”, but:

> I measured a self-built autonomous software factory, discovered it was spending over one
> million tokens per accepted task, identified the architectural causes, and redesigned its
> assurance economics without weakening correctness.

Artiom explicitly confirmed on 22 July 2026 that this claim is true and useful for his career.
Treat the current measurements as the before-state, not as proof that the redesign has already
succeeded. The eventual case study must show reproducible before/after token use, throughput,
acceptance/rejection rates, quality gates and defect-detection evidence. Do not publish the
“without weakening correctness” conclusion until those comparisons pass.

This extends [[2026-07-18-degree-and-scafad]] and constrains the open-ended spending permission
in [[2026-07-18-budget-doctrine]]: orchestrator/JAA expenditure remains defensible as career
capital when it produces external proof, demonstrable skill growth, and a measured efficiency
case study—not merely more private complexity.
