# Humanizer audit of the reconstruction draft

Question: **What makes the draft still read as AI-generated?**

- It is too symmetrical. Every chapter introduces a doctrine, gives a benefit and then gives a
  warning. The pattern becomes visible.
- Several paragraphs explain a point cleanly without showing how it felt or sounded in the
  office.
- The draft compresses the notebook so heavily that names, slogans, development routines and
  pitch details lose their texture.
- Some lines sound manufactured for quotation, especially "Keep the skills. Audit the
  opportunity."
- The chapter endings land too neatly.
- The tension is stated repeatedly instead of allowed to emerge from concrete material.
- The voice is calmer and more editorial than the source. Artiom's notes are impatient,
  sometimes profane and willing to leave an awkward thought unresolved.

## Final-pass decisions

- Open with the night the book was first assembled and the author's reason for leaving.
- Let notebook phrases appear as dated fragments, then test them against experience.
- Break the repeated benefit-versus-danger structure.
- Restore Birmingham, Saleshub, Appco, Phil Reid, Theo, Nicci Flynn, GOSH and Acorns where
  the source supports them.
- Keep useful uncertainty. Do not pretend every acronym or doctrine survived with a complete
  definition.
- Replace generic chapter summaries with practical exercises, cautions or abrupt endings.
- Remove all em and en dashes from final prose.
