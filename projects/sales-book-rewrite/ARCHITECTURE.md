# Replacement architecture

Working title: **Art of Sales: Notes from Five Months in Direct Sales**

## Preface: why I kept the notes

Five months of notes from offices, meetings, road trips and field work. The author has left
Saleshub and Appco. The book preserves what worked, records what the system did to people and
passes the useful parts on without pretending the experience was cleaner than it was.

## 1. What direct sales actually sells

Direct and indirect marketing; face-to-face, telephone, B2B, residential and events; exposure
and clients; performance pay; the ladder from job to self-employment, ownership and investment;
why the opportunity appeals; why the same opportunity story can become leverage over workers.

## 2. The field mindset

The three commandments: work ethic, student mentality and positive attitude. Detaching money
from the approach. Controlling what can be controlled. Indifference versus desperation.
Competition, self-talk, the skill gap and the cost of turning endurance into identity.

## 3. The five-step pitch

Introduction, presentation, short story, close and rehash. First impression, message and
sign-up process. SEE, PQA, tonality, speed, volume, questions and rapport. Explain what each
piece is meant to change in the interaction.

## 4. Probability, numbers and honest review

The law of averages; quality and quantity; no prejudging; putting full effort into enough
attempts; tracking sales and interactions; recording what happened after a deal; cross
referencing; questions, what went well and areas for improvement. Distinguish statistical
discipline from using volume to ignore poor technique.

## 5. Objections, closes and consent

Blocks, excuses and concerns; Feel Felt Found; agree, acknowledge, ignore, resume, close;
common residential objections from the Cycle of Development; indifference; sheep factor and
impulse. Explain the line between helping someone decide and pushing them past a real refusal.

## 6. How to learn faster than the office can teach you

Mentor and student roles; information seeker versus receiver; office, coaches meeting, prime
time and field; one-to-ones, calls, office visits, breakfasts and industry events; travelling
to stronger people; asking annoying questions; the skill gap; observable practice and review.

## 7. Training people and building a culture

Copyable systems, the staged new-starter plan, impacts, team activities, road trips and
accountability. What a mentor owes a student. How culture raises performance. How the same
tools can create conformity, fake scarcity and pressure to work beyond one's own interests.

## 8. Goals, value and progression

Sales, development, personal and career goals; who sets, writes and reviews them; GRASP; the
eight concepts of success; the eight daily steps; value versus immediate earnings; plans,
targets and the author's move toward his own business.

## 9. Two pitches from the field

The GOSH Children's Cancer Centre pitch and the Acorns Hospice pitch as dated case studies.
Break down their opening, problem, solution, human detail, social proof, price comparison and
close. Mark every historical statistic for verification. Examine when emotion clarifies the
stakes and when it becomes coercion.

## Conclusion: keep the skills, audit the system

What the five months changed. Why the author left. A practical warning about commission-only
offices, MLMs and pyramid structures. A reader should leave able to sell, but also able to see
when they themselves are being sold an opportunity.
