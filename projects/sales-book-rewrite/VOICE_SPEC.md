# Voice specification

## The voice in the notes

The notes are blunt, compressed and practical. They use imperatives, internal terminology,
short questions and hard contrasts. They are strongest when they record a mechanism in a few
words: "detach your money from your approach", "be an information seeker, not an information
receiver", "your value is your result", "plan your perfect day".

The later 4 November addition introduces a second voice: the former participant looking back
at the system with suspicion. That tension is the book's real character. Direct sales taught
discipline, observation and emotional control. The same environment also used dreams,
identity and group culture to extract longer hours. The rewrite must keep both sides.

## Rules for the rewrite

- Use British English.
- Write in first person for lived experience and clearly marked judgement.
- Use second person only for field instructions that come from the notes.
- Prefer concrete scenes, mechanisms and examples over abstract claims.
- Keep the internal vocabulary, then explain it in plain English.
- Attribute doctrine: "we were taught", "the office called this", or "my notes record".
- Do not invent customer stories, mentors' biographies or successful deals.
- Do not turn slogans into scientific laws.
- Separate effective technique from ethical approval.
- Preserve occasional roughness and profanity when it carries the author's actual intensity.
- Avoid motivational-speaker theatre and generic corporate language.
- Use sentence-case headings.
- Do not use em dashes or en dashes in final prose.
- Do not use decorative bolding, emojis, fake quotations or chatbot transitions.
- Do not end every chapter with an upbeat summary.

## Humanizer audit targets

The final pass must remove significance inflation, rule-of-three padding, `-ing` tail phrases,
generic conclusions, title-case headings, promotional adjectives, repeated "not just X but Y"
constructions, fake intimacy and manufactured punchlines.

It must not remove the human signals already present: specific dates, names, Birmingham,
Saleshub, Appco, GOSH, Acorns, the Daysaver comparison, the internal acronyms, mixed feelings,
rough syntax in quoted notebook fragments and the author's change of mind.
